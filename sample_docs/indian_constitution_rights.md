# Indian Constitution — Fundamental Rights (overview)

## What they are
Fundamental Rights are the basic human rights guaranteed to all citizens of
India by Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Constitution. They are justiciable,
meaning a citizen can approach the courts directly for their enforcement.

## The six rights
1. **Right to Equality** (Articles 14–18): equality before law, prohibition of
   discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth,
   and abolition of untouchability (Article 17).
2. **Right to Freedom** (Articles 19–22): speech and expression, assembly,
   association, movement, residence, and profession; protection against arbitrary
   arrest (Article 22).
3. **Right against Exploitation** (Articles 23–24): prohibition of human
   trafficking, forced labour, and child labour under 14 in hazardous work.
4. **Right to Freedom of Religion** (Articles 25–28).
5. **Cultural and Educational Rights** (Articles 29–30): minorities may conserve
   their language and culture and run educational institutions.
6. **Right to Constitutional Remedies** (Article 32): the right to move the
   Supreme Court to enforce the other rights. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it the
   "heart and soul" of the Constitution.

## Reasonable restrictions
No right is absolute. Freedoms under Article 19, for example, can be restricted
on grounds like public order, morality, and national security.

## Amendability
In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), the Supreme Court held that Parliament
can amend Fundamental Rights but cannot destroy the Constitution's *basic
structure*.
