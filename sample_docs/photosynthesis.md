# Photosynthesis — concise study notes

## Overview
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria
convert light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose. It takes in carbon
dioxide and water, and releases oxygen as a byproduct. The overall equation is:
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2.

## Where it happens
In plants, photosynthesis occurs mainly in the leaves, inside organelles called
chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, which
absorbs light — mostly in the red and blue wavelengths, reflecting green.

## Two stages
1. **Light-dependent reactions** (thylakoid membranes): light energy splits
   water (photolysis), releasing oxygen. Energy is captured as ATP and NADPH.
2. **Calvin cycle / light-independent reactions** (stroma): ATP and NADPH drive
   carbon fixation — CO2 is converted into glucose. This stage does not need
   light directly but depends on the products of stage 1.

## Key factors affecting the rate
- **Light intensity**: rate rises with intensity until another factor limits it.
- **CO2 concentration**: more CO2 generally increases the rate, up to saturation.
- **Temperature**: enzymes of the Calvin cycle work best around 25–35°C; too hot
  denatures them. These three are called *limiting factors*.

## Why it matters
Photosynthesis underpins almost all life: it produces the oxygen we breathe and
is the entry point of energy into nearly every food chain.
